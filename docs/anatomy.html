The anatomy of a creatura

Creatura is an agent framework for building agents out of regulation patterns taken from how living things and minds keep themselves on course. The core is one generic agent: a loop, a control layer between the model and its tools, and a contract for worlds that can lie. Everything else plugs in. A design is a regulator that fills the loop's slots, a behaviour is an extension that hooks into its verbs, and a world is where both are trained and tested.

Two plates: what the parts are and which of them talk, then what happens in one tick. Names in monospace are the types in the code.

The Latin

Five words, and why they are exact

Creatura creatūra, -ae · from creare, to bring forth
That which has been made. First the whole created order, then each made thing: so the singular names the framework and one agent alike. The learned form of Spanish criatura.
Monstrum monstrum, -i · from monere, to warn; root of monstrare, to show
A sign, a portent: the thing that appears in order to point at something. The trace is what a creatura shows; a monster is only a creature that has become a message.
Augur augur, -uris · the reader of signs
The one who reads what the creatura shows and answers yes or no, with standing but without hands. The Hotz critic: it cites or stays silent, earns the right to interrupt, and never acts.
Bestiarium bestiārium, -i · medieval, a book of beasts
The catalogue of creature designs and switches by name, each with its nature described. Never called a registry: that word belongs to the world's facts.
Vessel English, after vas electionis · a person as a chosen vessel
The hardware and the model a creatura inhabits. Kept in English because Spanish vas means "you go"; the Latin stays as etymology.
